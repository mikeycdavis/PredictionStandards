## What

Make the pack's verdict readable by a machine that is not this repository, and verify the pack
the same way twice — locally in a container and on a hosted runner — so that a claim about a
commit means the same thing wherever it is made.

## Why

Until this cycle the pack could be read by a person and only guessed at by a program. A consumer
had to infer a single disposition from a list of records, had no written contract for the document
it was parsing, and could be handed a truncated one without either side noticing. An external
enforcer arriving at that surface would have had to reimplement judgement the evaluator had already
made — and two evaluators drift.

## Notes

This initiative was **earned rather than scheduled**. ST-13 closed v1.1 at the tag with no v1.2
backlog opened, on the precedent that releases follow new evidence. The evidence arrived: an
external consumer that did not previously exist, and a delivery defect that only reproduced when
the pipeline was run somewhere new.

Reconstructed after the fact, from shipped evidence, on 2026-08-26 — deliberately not before the
tag existed. Writing the release story as `COMPLETE` while the tag did not yet exist would have put
a claim in this backlog ahead of its evidence, which is the one thing it has never done.
