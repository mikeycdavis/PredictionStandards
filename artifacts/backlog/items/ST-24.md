## What

Normalise `--as-of` on accept with `new Date(value).toISOString()`, restoring two invariants: every
emitted instant conforms to the published schema, and every date comparison uses the canonical UTC
day.

## Why

`--as-of` accepted any instant `Date.parse` understood and carried the caller's **text** through to
evaluation. The evaluation day is taken positionally and handed to exception and attestation expiry,
which compare ISO dates as strings, so the same moment spelled two ways produced two verdicts:

    2026-08-09T23:00:00-05:00   passed 31  failed 4
    2026-08-10T04:00:00Z        passed 30  failed 5

An exception lapsing that night was honoured for a run that happened after it lapsed.

## Notes

The schema half was reported by an automated review of the release candidate; the day defect was
found underneath it and **predates the schema entirely** — record staleness converts with `ms()` and
was always offset-correct, so nothing downstream noticed. Every `asOf` in the suite was a `Z`
literal, which is why the branch had never been exercised.

Rejecting offsets and widening the schema pattern were both rejected: the first breaks callers to
fix something a conversion handles, the second silences the validator and leaves the day wrong.

The boundary test reads a **verdict** rather than an echoed timestamp, because an echoed timestamp
only proves what the tool printed — a display-only half-fix would pass it otherwise. One visible
consequence: an `--as-of` given to whole seconds now echoes with explicit milliseconds, which is the
form the default path always emitted.
