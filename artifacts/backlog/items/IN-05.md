## What

Establish what a policy's declared `standardVersion` means, whether the evaluator can resolve it,
and what follows when it cannot — so that the framework version a policy names is a checked claim
rather than a field carried through to the report untouched.

## Why

`schemas/project-policy.schema.json` already makes a behavioural promise about this field: *"An
unresolvable version is a configuration error, not a compliance failure."* Nothing in the pack
resolves the value, so the promise has never been true. A policy may name any version at all —
one that never existed, one not yet released, or one whose rules differ from the rules actually
applied — and every run reports it back as though it had been honoured.

## Notes

Admitted on 2026-08-27 on evidence specific to this defect, not by adjacency to the v1.2.0 cycle.
The hold condition recorded in ADR 0010 — *"until it independently earns entry on its own
evidence"* — was met by four things: the schema's stated promise; production code that never
resolves the value; a demonstrated instance of the failure, this repository evaluating
`1.1.0`-introduced rules while its own policy named `1.0.0`; and
`artifacts/release-review/2026-08-25-policy-standard-version-semantics.md`, which identified this
as a separate future cycle rather than fixing it during release preparation and named itself as
the evidence that cycle would start from.

The three candidates held alongside it stay held. F2 — the stale-digest falsy read at
`scripts/compliance.mjs:459` — was **not** admitted with it: this initiative earning entry does not
satisfy F2's hold, and the two are predicted to overlap in `compliance.mjs`, which is a further
reason not to admit both reflexively.
