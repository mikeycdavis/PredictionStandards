## What

Establish what the cycle was worth, move the release identity, fix what reviewing the candidate
found, then freeze, verify from `main`, and tag.

## Why

A release number is a claim about compatibility. Deciding it from the repository's actual
commitments rather than from a version field that happened to be nearby is what makes the claim
checkable.

## Notes

1.2.0 was chosen as an **artifact/API minor**: the pack became interoperable, and the normative
catalog did not move — 19 standards, 52 rules, every protection attribute byte-identical to
v1.1.0. The major reading was considered and recorded rather than discarded.

Four candidates held from earlier cycles stayed held through this release and are **not**
represented here: the CI-teardown ownership standard, the non-squash merge-topology convention as
a written convention, the retroactive `v1.0.0` tag, and the stale-digest falsy read at
`scripts/compliance.mjs:459`. A new release cycle is not evidence for any of them.
