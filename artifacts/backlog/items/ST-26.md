## What

Decide what `policy.standardVersion` asserts and how the evaluator establishes whether that
assertion holds, and state the falsifier for each case rather than leaving the semantics to be
inferred from an implementation.

At least these cases must be answered, each with the observation that would show the answer wrong:

- **Current self-policy** — this repository's own `project-policy.yml`, now naming `1.2.0`.
- **Intentionally pinned older policy** — a legitimately frozen adoption that names an earlier
  version and must not be broken by this work.
- **A nonexistent or future version** — a value that names no release the pack has made.
- **A malformed version** — a value the schema's semver pattern does not admit.
- **The relationship to `introducedIn`** — whether, and how, a policy's declared version bears on
  rules introduced after it.

## Why

Every one of those cases currently produces the same outcome: the value is passed through to the
report and nothing is established. Deciding them together is what prevents the resolution rule from
being shaped by whichever case was implemented first.

## Notes

**Do not pre-decide that a policy must equal the current `VERSION`.** The adoption archives
`artifacts/adoption/project-policy.yml`, `02-project-policy.yml` and `03-project-policy.yml` all
pin `1.0.0` and are frozen deliberately; a rule requiring equality would declare the pack's own
evidence base a configuration error.

The `introducedIn` case is the one with a concrete precedent already in the repository. Every rule
carries the field, and exactly two rules were introduced after `1.0.0` —
`falsifiability.declared` and `falsifiability.resolution-not-self-determined`, both `1.1.0`. Those
two are the demonstrated failure: this repository evaluated them while its own policy named
`1.0.0`, for two releases, and nothing noticed. Whether the correct response is to refuse, to
narrow the catalog to the declared version, or to report the discrepancy as not-evaluated is
exactly what this story decides.

Scope stops at the decision. Implementation is a separate story and is not filed, because its shape
depends on this outcome.

The decision was **revised before merge**. The first draft resolved a version against a numeric
window, `maxIntroducedIn(catalog) <= declared <= VERSION`, and review found that a numeric interval
is not a release register: `1.1.1` sits inside `[1.1.0, 1.2.0]` and names no release the pack has
made. The draft had substituted an upper bound for proof of existence, and had read an unchanged
`introducedIn` as proof of compatibility — but `introducedIn` records when a rule was *added* and
nothing records when one was *changed*. ADR 0012 now separates existence, compatibility and the
introduction bound into three establishments that must all hold, and carries the counterexample and
the correction rather than smoothing them away. This story stays COMPLETE: the gap was in the
decision and was closed inside it.
